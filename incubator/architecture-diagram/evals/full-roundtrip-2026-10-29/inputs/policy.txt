Drawing conventions for an isolated architecture generation experiment.

The supplied immediate description is the sole scenario input. Preserve architectural elements, types, responsibilities, call initiators, protocols, domain memberships, conditions, multiplicity, qualifiers and architecture-bearing notes. Never invent confirmations, capabilities or missing details. The description is a resolved synthetic fixture, not a claim about a real deployment. Unknowns stated by the description must remain unknown. Do not research the scenario or consult any earlier diagram or source case.

Deliver one editable, uncompressed .drawio page and a real browser-rendered PNG/SVG. Title: Dynamic diagram (deployment view): <scenario>. Use C4 type/technology captions and nested Deployment node / Software system boundary groups. Use exact usable entries in the generic pinned catalog for official AWS/Azure service icons. Disambiguate duplicate names by palette/ID. Do not substitute a product's icon as a cloud vendor logo. If the pinned catalog lacks an appropriate official symbol, use a truthful plain box/text boundary rather than a fabricated or wrong-vendor icon; report that conformance limit. AWS AWS4 and Azure Azure2 only. Preserve catalog shape/color references; 72-unit service icons and captions below. General person glyphs/plain boxes are allowed. General typography is 12 points; bold boundary headings and failure labels. Native Misc > Note comments may fit text automatically and keep their native appearance. The exact Note style is:
shape=note;whiteSpace=wrap;html=1;backgroundOutline=1;autosizeText=1;fontColor=#000000;darkOpacity=0.05;fillColor=#FFF9B2;strokeColor=none;fillStyle=solid;fontSize=20;direction=west;gradientDirection=north;gradientColor=#FFF2A1;shadow=1;size=20;pointerEvents=1;
With HTML labels, encode line breaks as <br> before XML escaping. Tag comments with annotationKind="comment", annotationFor="<subject id>"; tag the legend group annotationKind="legend". Comments are not services or call endpoints. Put diagram-wide purpose below the title, local comments near their subjects, and a compact legend/table in a reserved area. Do not put authoring assumptions or evaluation caveats into the diagram; retain architectural facts/unknowns from the supplied input.

All relationships have one filled caller-to-callee arrowhead, unless a stated infrastructure forwarding leg is being depicted: label that leg clearly as traffic routing, not an application-originating call. Main calls are solid, auxiliary authentication/queries dashed. Fold normal responses into request labels; a separate failure response may have a bold label. Native connector labels sit on the line with white background and do not mask boundary outlines/titles. Show step numbers; parallel branches may use letter suffixes. Independent flows may use at most three color-blind-safe colors with A/B numbering and a legend. For described counts, draw representative groups with explicit counts/names; do not silently collapse multiple environments, queues or workflows to one.

Every boundary is an architectural domain. Keep a route entirely within all shared enclosing boundaries; cross only required domains once each. No escape/re-entry or unrelated-domain transit to avoid text. If traffic must pass an intermediate router/firewall/VPN, represent the intermediate element explicitly and split the forwarding path into appropriately labeled legs. Do not draw a direct logical edge whose detour visually implies unintended domain transit. Avoid captions/elements/annotation backgrounds, shared path segments and unnecessary bends; minimize line crossings. Prefer changing node placement, spacing or page dimensions over violating semantics. Use a page up to about 3800 x 2600, as needed; do not shrink text to make a dense graph fit.
