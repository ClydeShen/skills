# Research: Bundled icon rights need explicit notices and unresolved-use decisions

Date: 2026-11-06. Question: can the Architecture Diagram package redistribute its bundled artwork under the owner's newly approved MIT license? Context: first-release preparation. Scope: the bundled `vendor-icons.json` sources, not a legal opinion or completed release approval.

Conclusion: **no blanket MIT declaration**. [official] The SVG supplier separates its MIT package code from each icon's upstream license. Microsoft grants specific architecture-use rights for Fabric/Azure collections, while several other policies reserve logo permissions. [inference] Add package-local third-party notices and applicable license texts, verify output-attribution handling, and resolve uncertain assets before release. A catalog field or generic trademark disclaimer does not establish permission.

## Inventory and coverage

[measured] The inspected [vendor catalog](../../../incubator/architecture-diagram/candidate/architecture-diagram/assets/vendor-icons.json) contains 94 entries: 21 labelled MIT, 39 CC0-1.0, 22 Fabric restricted-use, three Apache-2.0, one CC-BY-SA-3.0, one CC-BY-4.0 and seven with brand-use/fair-use/trademark/no-license descriptions. These counts describe supplier metadata, not independent clearance. VMware and Splunk have `viewBoxTrimmed: true`; other entries have no recorded artwork modification.

The source families are `@thesvg/icons@4.0.0`, `@fabric-msft/svg-icons@11.1.0`, and six pinned repositories: A2A, Google ADK, AutoGen, Semantic Kernel, Microsoft Agent Framework and Weaviate's website. Package/source licenses and relevant primary policies were inspected below. Every supplier-labelled MIT/CC0 logo's original grant and current brand policy were **not** independently established. In particular, supplier metadata and a brand owner's policy can differ.

The draw.io catalog primarily identifies library styles and paths; it is not this bundled artwork inventory. Rights in a separately obtained viewer, libraries, output images and included third-party artwork remain distinct. This note does not certify the viewer's complete dependency/license tree.

## Minimal implementation and acceptance

1. Scope the approved MIT license to original skill instructions/scripts and original documentation. Preserve third-party exceptions explicitly.
2. Add a package-local third-party notice with asset IDs/source revisions, owners, supplied licenses, modification status, applicable terms and unresolved statuses. Use the catalog as the inventory; do not create a second manually maintained artwork catalog.
3. Include required MIT/Apache and relevant CC license material. Retain applicable original copyright and NOTICE text; do not invent holders for supplier entries that lack them.
4. Define how attribution follows generated `.drawio`, PNG and companion files where required. Test standalone delivery, not only an installed-package notice. For example: `Jenkins logo: Jenkins project (https://www.jenkins.io/), CC BY-SA 3.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/); supplied artwork unchanged by this package.` This wording must not conceal earlier supplier transformations.
5. For unverified or incompatible entries, obtain permission/qualified clarification or make an explicit authorized exclusion/replacement. Plain boxes are the package's existing fallback. Runtime downloading does not remove downstream permission obligations.
6. Re-run icon lookup/embedding and behavioral comparisons after any inventory change. A notice-only change cannot prove output compliance; a new hash cannot be approved just to clear a gate.

[inference] Restricting or excluding uncertain artwork is a product change, not a license-text substitution. It may be the simplest first-release treatment, but must preserve honest coverage claims and pass the relevant regression review. No artwork or runtime behavior was changed by this research.

## Not verified and owner decisions

Not verified: exact upstream rights for every supplier-labelled MIT/CC0 icon; permission for the identified reserved marks; whether any given fair-use exception applies in the intended jurisdictions; legal derivative-versus-collection treatment; compliance of standalone generated images; viewer dependency clearance. The observations above are not an infringement finding or a legal certification.

The owner already approved MIT for original content. Remaining decisions concern resolving uncertain marks through permission/qualified review versus excluding/replacing them, and how required attribution accompanies generated deliverables. These decisions cannot be replaced by an agent asserting that a generic disclaimer cures all rights.

Confidence: high that the blanket-MIT/notice-only approach is insufficient, 3.5/4 (owner direction 1, primary evidence 1, reasoning 1, local execution history 0.5). Completeness of asset-by-asset legal clearance remains unverified.
